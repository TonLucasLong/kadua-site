Psychologist, psychiatrist, or counselor: who do I need?

Information, not diagnosis or treatment. This guide was drafted from the sources listed at the end and has not yet been reviewed by a qualified mental health adviser. Review status: pending. Last updated 2026-10-25. If you are in danger right now, go to Immediate Help.

Most people's first question isn't "what do I have." It's "who do I even talk to." Here's the short version for the Philippines.

Psychiatrist

A medical doctor who specialized in mental health. Psychiatrists can assess, diagnose, and prescribe medication, and some also do talk therapy. Go here first if you think medication might be part of the answer, if symptoms are severe or sudden, or if a psychologist has suggested a medical evaluation.

Psychologist

A licensed professional trained in assessment and psychotherapy. In the Philippines, psychologists are licensed by the Professional Regulation Commission under the Philippine Psychology Act of 2009. They don't prescribe medication. Go here for talk therapy, assessment, and help working through patterns, stress, grief, anxiety, or relationships.

Guidance counselor

Licensed under the Guidance and Counseling Act of 2004, usually working in schools and universities. A good first stop for students: they're free on campus, they know the school's resources, and they can refer you onward.

Social worker and community-based support

Registered social workers help with the practical side: family situations, benefits, referrals to public services, and case management. Many LGU health centers and NGOs offer this at no cost.

Which first?

  • Student with a campus counselor available: start there.
  • Want to talk things through, no medical red flags: psychologist.
  • Severe symptoms, big changes in sleep or appetite, thoughts of self-harm, or you suspect medication could help: psychiatrist, and if you're in danger now, Immediate Help.
  • Money is the barrier: ask about sliding-scale fees, check your city health office, PhilHealth mental health packages, or university psychology clinics that offer supervised low-cost sessions.

Check the license

Any psychologist, psychiatrist (as a physician), or guidance counselor should have a PRC license you can look up. Ask for the license number; it's a normal question and a good sign if they answer easily. You can verify it on the PRC's online verification service.

Sources: Republic Act No. 10029, Philippine Psychology Act of 2009; Republic Act No. 9258, Guidance and Counseling Act of 2004; Republic Act No. 11036, Mental Health Act of 2018; Professional Regulation Commission, online license verification (https://online.prc.gov.ph/verification).
