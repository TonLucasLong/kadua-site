Everyday self-care that costs nothing

Information, not diagnosis or treatment. This guide was drafted from the sources listed at the end and has not yet been reviewed by a qualified mental health adviser. Review status: pending. Last updated 2026-10-25. If you are in danger right now, go to Immediate Help.

Self-care gets sold as candles and spa days. The versions with the most evidence behind them are boring, free, and small enough to do on a bad day. This isn't a treatment plan. It's a floor to stand on while you get one.

Five small moves

  1. Anchor your wake-up time. Same time every day, even after a bad night. Sleep is easier to fix from the morning end.
  2. Get daylight early. Ten minutes outside, or by a bright window, soon after waking.
  3. Move a little, daily. A ten-minute walk counts. The point is regular, not impressive.
  4. One real conversation. Message or see one person you trust, not about anything in particular. Connection is protective.
  5. Ground yourself when it spikes. The WHO's stress guide teaches a simple routine: notice what you're thinking, slow your breathing, name five things you can see, and come back to what you were doing. It's a skill, so it gets easier.

What to notice

Two weeks or more of most of these, most days: sleep way off, no interest in things you used to like, hard to function at school or work, pulling away from everyone, or thoughts of not wanting to be here. That's the point to stop managing alone and talk to a professional. If the last one is happening now, go to Immediate Help.

What self-care is not

Not a reason to skip help you need. Not a performance. Not a replacement for medication someone has prescribed. If a product promises to fix anxiety or depression on its own, that promise is the problem.

Sources: World Health Organization. (2020). Doing what matters in times of stress: An illustrated guide (https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927); World Health Organization, Mental health fact sheets on depression and physical activity (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets).
