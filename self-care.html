Self-care, the practical kind

Short guides you can read in five minutes and use today. They cover everyday wellbeing and how to use support well. They are information, not diagnosis or treatment, and they never replace a professional.

Guides

  • Choosing · 5 min

    Psychologist, psychiatrist, or counselor: who do I need?

    What each one does, who they're for, and how to check a license.

  • Starting · 4 min

    What to expect in a first session

    How to prepare, what gets asked, and what a good fit feels like.

  • Hotlines · 4 min

    Before you call a hotline

    What the call is like, what to have ready, and what to do if you can't get through.

  • Everyday · 5 min

    Everyday self-care that costs nothing

    Sleep, movement, daylight, routine, and people: small moves with evidence behind them.

  • For a friend · 5 min

    How to support someone who's struggling

    Listening without fixing, asking the hard question, and knowing when to call.

  • How these are written

    Reviewed, dated, plain

    Each guide is drafted from public health guidance and cited where it makes a factual claim. A qualified mental health adviser reads it before it is marked reviewed; unreviewed guides say so at the top.

Self-care products, later

A small line of simple items (a guided journal, a calm kit) is planned for a later phase, only if enough people want it and only after suppliers, packaging, and delivery are confirmed. Nothing Kadua sells will ever be presented as a replacement for professional treatment.
